What is the difference between Balinese, Thai and shiatsu massage?

The main differences are oil, movement and pressure. Balinese massage uses aromatic oil with flowing strokes, acupressure and gentle stretching. Thai massage is oil-free and combines firm pressure with assisted stretching. Shiatsu, from Japan, is also oil-free and uses finger, thumb and palm pressure on specific points, with gentle stretching [1][3][5].

What is a Balinese massage?

Balinese massage is a full-body massage from Bali that combines long, flowing strokes, acupressure, skin rolling and gentle stretching with aromatic oils. Its roots are usually traced to village healers, called balian, blended with Indian and Chinese influences such as aromatic oils and pressure-point work [1]. One 2026 guide calls it the best entry point for first-timers [2].

At Spa Bali Moon, our Balinese massage uses steady pressure, gentle stretching and aromatic oils, and the therapist adapts the pressure and focus areas to you. Sessions are 1 hour (IDR 159.000), 1.5 hours (IDR 239.000) or 2 hours (IDR 330.000) per person. For its history and techniques, read What Is a Balinese Massage?.

What does a Balinese massage feel like?

Expect a calm, rhythmic massage. You are given a clean towel or spa attire, and only the area being worked on is uncovered. Massage oil lets the strokes glide, the pressure is steady rather than intense, and you can ask for lighter or firmer pressure at any time.

What is a Thai massage?

Thai massage, or Nuad Thai, is a traditional manual therapy from Thailand, listed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2019 [3]. Practitioners use their hands, elbows, knees and feet to apply pressure and guide the body into stretches [3][4]. You usually stay clothed, often on a floor mat, and it is sometimes called assisted yoga [4].

At Spa Bali Moon, Thai massage is oil-free and combines firm pressure from the therapist's hands, thumbs, palms and elbows with assisted stretching, so wear comfortable clothing you can move in. Sessions are 30 minutes (IDR 133.000), 1 hour (IDR 259.000) or 1.5 hours (IDR 379.000) per person. Read more in Thai Massage Benefits & Techniques Explained.

Is Thai massage painful?

Thai massage can feel firm or intense, but it should not cause sharp or severe pain. Tell your therapist straight away if a stretch or the pressure feels too strong, and they will adjust it. Small studies suggest Thai massage may help with some back pain and tension headaches, but the research is limited [4].

What is shiatsu massage?

Shiatsu is a Japanese bodywork technique whose name means "finger pressure". The practitioner presses points along the body with thumbs, fingers and palms, holding each for several seconds, often with gentle stretches. No oil is used, and you can usually stay in comfortable clothing [5][6]. It has been regulated as a health profession in Japan since the mid-twentieth century [5].

At Spa Bali Moon, shiatsu is oil-free pressure-point work with fingers, thumbs and palms and gentle stretching, always adjusted to your comfort. Sessions are 30 minutes (IDR 119.000), 1 hour (IDR 219.000) or 1.5 hours (IDR 329.000) per person. Thirty minutes suits neck and shoulder tension or a first try.

Is shiatsu the same as Japanese massage?

At Spa Bali Moon, yes: our Japanese massage is shiatsu, the best-known Japanese massage technique. Some studies suggest shiatsu may help with chronic low back pain and sleep quality, but placebo-controlled trials are hard to design, and the evidence is still limited [5].

Are Balinese, Thai and shiatsu massage safe?

For most healthy adults, yes. Massage appears to carry few risks when given by a trained practitioner [7]. Tell your therapist about any health conditions, injuries or recent surgery before you start, and speak up if anything hurts. If you are unsure whether massage suits you, check with your doctor first [7].

Stretch-based styles need extra care. A medically reviewed guide advises checking with a doctor before Thai massage if you have heart disease, high blood pressure, osteoporosis or another spinal condition, diabetes or recent surgery, and does not recommend it during pregnancy [4]. Shiatsu should not be applied over open wounds, inflamed skin, bruises or healing fractures, and is not recommended for people prone to blood clots [5].
