# What Helps a Bad Sunburn in Bali?

For a bad sunburn, get out of the sun, cool the skin with a cool shower or damp towel, apply aftersun or an unperfumed moisturiser, drink plenty of water and take paracetamol or ibuprofen if needed, following the leaflet [1]. ## What should I do first for a bad sunburn?

Start straight away: the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) says to begin treating sunburn as soon as you notice it [2]. The NHS and the AAD recommend these steps [1][2]:

1. **Get out of the sun**, ideally indoors [1][2].
2. **Cool the skin** with a cool shower, bath or damp towel. Take care that babies and young children do not get too cold [1].
3. **Moisturise** with aftersun or an unperfumed moisturiser [1]. The AAD suggests a moisturiser containing aloe vera or soy, applied while the skin is still damp [2].
4. **Drink plenty of water and avoid alcohol** [1]. Sunburn draws fluid to the skin's surface and away from the rest of the body, so extra water helps prevent dehydration [2].
5. **Take pain relief** such as paracetamol or ibuprofen, following the leaflet [1]. A pharmacist can advise on the best treatment and whether you need a doctor [1].
6. **Keep the burn covered** from direct sun until it has healed [1].

## What should I avoid on sunburnt skin?

The NHS advises you not to [1]:

- put ice or ice packs on the skin
- pop blisters
- scratch or peel off flaking skin
- wear tight clothing over the burn
- use petroleum jelly on sunburnt skin

Advice on petroleum jelly differs between sources. The AAD suggests it to protect blisters while they heal [2], and Cleveland Clinic suggests it once the skin has cooled [3]. If you are unsure, ask a pharmacist.

## When is a sunburn serious enough to see a doctor?

Most mild sunburn can be cared for at home, but some signs need medical help. Cleveland Clinic advises seeking medical treatment immediately for blisters over more than 20% of the body (such as a whole leg, the entire back or both arms), severe swelling, chills, extreme pain, a fever above 39°C, signs of dehydration or infection, or sunburn in a baby under one year old [3].

The NHS adds feeling very tired, dizzy or sick, and a headache or muscle cramps [1]. Healthdirect Australia lists large areas of blistering, signs of infection such as fever or yellow crusts, severe pain, and signs of dehydration such as dizziness, passing less urine and tiredness [4]. ### What about babies and children?

Babies and children have more delicate skin than adults and burn more easily, and sunscreen is not recommended for babies under six months [4]. The NHS says a baby or young child with sunburn needs urgent medical advice [1], and Cleveland Clinic lists any sunburn in a baby under one year old as a reason to seek treatment immediately [3].

### Is it sunburn or heat exhaustion?

A long day in the sun can cause both. Signs of heat exhaustion include tiredness, dizziness, headache, nausea, heavy sweating with pale, clammy skin, cramps, a high temperature and strong thirst [5]. The NHS advises moving the person somewhere cooler, removing extra clothing, giving water or a rehydration drink and cooling the skin; they should start to feel better within 30 minutes [5].

If they are still unwell after 30 minutes, or show confusion, hot skin that is not sweating, fast breathing, a seizure or loss of consciousness, treat it as an emergency [5]. In Indonesia, the ambulance number is 118 [8].

## How long does a bad sunburn take to heal?

Sunburn usually gets better within seven days, and peeling often starts a few days after the burn [1]. Pain tends to peak about 24 hours after the burn, and more severe sunburn takes longer to heal [3]. If your skin is not improving, or new symptoms appear, see a doctor.

## Does aloe vera help sunburn?

Aloe vera can make sunburnt skin feel more comfortable, but it is not a cure. The AAD suggests a moisturiser containing aloe vera or soy to soothe sunburnt skin [2], and Cleveland Clinic lists aloe vera gel among cooling, hydrating gels for home care [3]. Healthdirect Australia notes that aloe vera may feel soothing but does not speed up healing, and may sometimes irritate the skin [4]. If a product stings or causes a rash, stop using it.

## Why do people burn so quickly in Bali?

The World Health Organization (WHO) explains that the closer you are to the equator, the higher the UV levels, and that water and sand reflect UV and raise exposure further [6]. UV can also be high under cloud [6].

The Denpasar regional office of Indonesia's weather agency (BBMKG Wilayah III) has warned that UV exposure in Bali can be very high, most likely between 11am and 2pm local time, especially in the dry season when there is little cloud [7]. The WHO recommends sun protection whenever the UV index reaches 3 or above [6].

## How can I protect my skin while it heals?

Keep sunburnt skin covered from direct sun until it has healed [1]. Give your skin time to recover before you spend long hours outdoors again.

When you do go out, the AAD recommends seeking shade, wearing protective clothing and using a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher on uncovered skin [2]. BMKG Denpasar also advises limiting time in direct sun between 10am and 4pm, wearing a wide-brimmed hat and UV-blocking sunglasses, and reapplying SPF 30+ every two hours, even on cloudy days and after swimming or sweating [7].

See a doctor if blisters cover a large area, or if you have severe swelling, extreme pain, fever, chills, dizziness, signs of dehydration or signs of infection such as pus [3][4].
